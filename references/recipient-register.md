# Recipient register

Match the register to the reader's role. Identify the recipient's role before drafting. When it is unclear, ask one question.

## Recruiter or coordinator

Owns the process, not the decision. Wants a clear yes or no, times, and the attachment. Register: efficient and friendly. A three-line message gets three lines back. Scheduling replies skip the review loop. Do not pitch the recruiter on fit; they move the paper.

Stakes tier: everyone else.

## Hiring manager

Owns the decision. Gets the plain statement of interest and the ask for the next step. The recap in a follow-up is what they said about the team and the role. Full four moves. Treat concerns they already settled as settled; do not reopen them.

Stakes tier: decision-maker.

## Peer or cross-functional interviewer

Does not own the decision. Gets a lighter note: thanks for something specific they said, one line of shared ground, a warm close. No statement of interest in the role as if they could act on it. No ask for the next step. See `hard-rules.md`, rule 4.

Stakes tier: everyone else.

## Executive or skip-level

Owns or strongly shapes the decision and reads on a phone in ten seconds. Shorter than a hiring-manager note. One specific recap of what they said, one line on what interests the user about where the company is going, the ask, a close. No jargon.

Stakes tier: decision-maker.

## Contact, referrer, or alumni connection

Did the user a favor or may do one. Gets thanks for the specific favor, a one-line update on where things stand, and either the next ask or a plain "I will let you know how it goes." Keep them in the loop after the outcome, both ways.

Stakes tier: everyone else.

## Panel

Apply the register per person. See `hard-rules.md`, rule 5: one note per panelist with a different recap.
