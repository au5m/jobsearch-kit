# Defaults

Neutral fallbacks for every pack section. Loaded when `profile.pack` is empty or names a folder that does not exist. Same headings as a pack, one block each, no role split. The content works in any field and names no industry, title, product category or verb pair that belongs to one profession.

## Vocabulary

### role: default

Use: led, built, ran, delivered, reduced, increased, improved, launched, resolved, designed, trained, owned (only when the source says so), supported (when the source says so).

Avoid: leveraged, spearheaded, synergized, utilized, facilitated, responsible for, helped with, assisted in, tasked with, participated in.

Shape every bullet as: what you did, to what, with what result. Keep the result measurable when the source has a number.

## Keyword families

### role: default

Read the posting and group its terms into: the core activity (what the job does most days), the tools or methods named, the scale or scope (team size, budget, volume, region), the audience or customer, and any credential. Match the posting's own words where the resume shows the same work. Do not add a term the resume does not support.

## Ask patterns

### role: default

After a conversation, the natural ask is one of: meet another person on the team, learn about the next stage, or an intro to someone who can speak to the day-to-day. Pick the one that fits what the person said. In outreach, ask for a time.

## Reviewer notes

### role: default

The reviewer plays a busy person in the recipient's role reading on a phone. They care whether the message is easy to answer, whether it recaps what they said, and whether it asks for one clear thing.

## Role lens

### role: default

When researching a company as a candidate, weigh: whether the work is the kind the user wants to do, who they would report to and how long that person has been there, how the team has grown or shrunk in the last 18 months, what the company says about the role's success in year one, and how stable the business is for the user's time horizon.

Questions to validate before joining: what does success look like at six and twelve months; why is the role open; who was in it before and where did they go; how are decisions made on the team; what is the review and promotion rhythm.

## Interview formats

### role: default

Common formats: screening call with a recruiter, hiring-manager conversation, panel with future peers, a take-home or work sample, a presentation of past work, a final conversation with a senior leader. Prepare for each by learning who is in the room and what the format is testing.

## Examples

### role: default

Before: Responsible for managing the weekly reporting process for the department.
After: Rebuilt the weekly report for a [team size] department, cutting prep time from [hours] to [hours] and ending [#] recurring data errors.

The after version names a scope, an action and a result. Every number is a marked blank until the source supplies it.
